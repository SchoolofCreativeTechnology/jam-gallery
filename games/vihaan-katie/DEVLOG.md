# Devlog

Your record of the jam, in your words. The devlog terminal (`claude --agent devlog`) helps you write it; you decide what goes in. On a team, initials show who decided.

## Entries

Format for each change (one change per entry):

### HH:MM: short title
- **Changed:**
- **Expected:**
- **Saw:**
- **Decided, and why (who):**

Format for each milestone check-in (M1 to M5):

### HH:MM: M# check-in
- **Expected:**
- **Saw when we played it:**
- **Next (who decided):**

Format for each playtest (after M2 and after M3):

### HH:MM: Playtest N
- **Their story:**
- **Bored or stuck:**

## Reflection

*We pretty much orchestrated the whole thing based on our idea and di not let the LLM drive us the other way round. I didnt even ask it for any ideas, and if it suggested something I outright rejected it and stcuk to my concept and what I planned for this game. I knew what was feasible and what's not and it worked out in the end even though it suggested it might not. 
For future game jams I wouldn't underestimate the amount of time we have, and make something even more feasible, not to say what we have right now isn't great. It's really good for the time, but if we did a 2d game with the same theme, it would've been even more polished and production ready. Also deploying multiple agents would be great next time (even more).*

## AI Use Disclosure

*We did all the sketches and ideation manually. We only used LLMs to code and debug, and used tools like Blender, Flow Studio, etc. to make the 3D models. Even the music was handpicked and open-sourced, not AI-generated. It was a great learning experience for both of us: me (a UX major) and my teammate (an ANIM major), as we're new to game dev, and this expanded our horizon of possibilities for future work.*

*Be accurate: neither more nor less than what happened.*
